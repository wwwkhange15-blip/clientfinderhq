How to follow up with a freelance client who stopped replying

Silence rarely means no. Most people are busy, and your message slid down their inbox. A short, friendly follow-up often gets the conversation moving again.

1. Wait three to four working days

Following up the next day feels pushy. Waiting more than a week lets the conversation go cold. Three to four working days is a fair gap for the first follow-up.

2. Reply to your own earlier email

Answer on the same thread so the client can see what you sent before. Keep it to two lines.

Hi Sara, just checking you saw my note about the customer stories. Happy to send the sample over if it's useful. No pressure either way.

3. Add something new in the second follow-up

If there is still no reply after about a week, don't repeat yourself. Add one small, useful thing, such as a short tip for their business or a relevant example of your work.

Hi Sara, one quick idea for your pricing page: customers often trust a short quote with a name and role more than a long paragraph. Here is a sample of what I mean: [link]. Would you like me to draft one for you?

4. Send a polite last message, then stop

A final note tells the client the door is open and removes any pressure. After this, stop. Sending more than three messages risks annoying the person and hurting your name.

Hi Sara, I'll assume the timing isn't right, so I won't keep following up. If you'd like help with customer stories in the future, just reply to this email. Wishing you a good quarter.

What to avoid

Don't guilt-trip ("I haven't heard back from you"), don't send a message every day, and don't write when you're frustrated. Repeated "just bumping this" emails also add nothing for the reader.

Keep track, then move on

Note the date of each message in a simple spreadsheet. If a client doesn't reply after the last message, move on to the next prospect. You can contact them again in two or three months with something new to share. Replies are never guaranteed, but a calm, steady process keeps your reputation good.
